One conference page, five ways

Five drafts of the template every past and future Schiller conference will be published on. One content spine, one set of dials, and five genuinely different rooms — because a template that cannot hold forty years of very differently-documented conferences is not a template, it is a design for one page.

The drafts

Five rooms, one spine

Each draft renders the same record. Switch the record with the strip at the foot of every page: Berlin 2025 (panels, deep-linked by the second), Beethoven 2024 (four panels, a cultural one among them), Berlin 2016 (one film per speech, plus a nine-work concert) and Strasbourg 2023 (a title, two dates and a city — nothing else).

  1. A The Proceedings limestone · the default

    The record, walkable. A sticky programme rail, the sessions unrolled, every talk a row that plays the tape at its own second. The aesthetically safe base the other four diverge from.

    Berlin 2016 the empty record

  2. B The Marquee night · no photographs at all

    The playbill: the title at poster scale, the sessions as sheets under a running numeral, the company as a ribbon of names, the concert as the finale. The answer to a conference with no usable image — which is most of them.

    the nine-work concert

  3. C The Rostrum paper · people first

    The conference told by its voices: every speaker a card with the house they speak from, and a portrait you press to hear them begin. Panels become filters. For the records where the roster is the strongest thing the archive holds.

    18 of 20 with a portrait

  4. D The Thread limestone · scroll-driven

    The conference as the time it took. Each session a band as long as it ran, each talk a mark at the minute it began, and a playhead that names whatever is under your eye and plays from exactly there.

    untimed sessions say so

  5. E The Atrium limestone · the densest

    The room with doors: a sticky sub-navigation over overview, programme, speakers, culture and recordings. The draft that carries a conference which has not happened yet — registration where the recordings will later sit.

    the upcoming state the empty record

The problem the dials solve

One template, and no two conferences alike

Every conference must feel like itself without an editor touching CSS, and without the large photographs the archive does not have. Five things carry the difference, all of them set in the record:

groundlimestone · paper · night — which Jasper surface the page sits on. A night page carries no second field.
the sealA medallion cut from a hash of the conference key: its ring count, tick count and phase are the conference's own. Drawn, never photographed — so every conference has a device and nobody has to find an image.
the titleSet to the paper: the more lines the record's own title needs, the smaller the cut. Nothing is ever truncated.
the tape's stillsYouTube's own frames, used small and tonal — the one image every filmed conference in this archive is guaranteed to have.
the shape of the recordPanels deep-linked by the second, one film per speech, a concert programme, or nothing at all: the adapter normalises all four and each draft is built to be complete with an empty programme.

The four records

Built from the archive, not from a mock-up

Every name, affiliation, talk title, timestamp, composer and conductor below was parsed from the descriptions the Institute published with its own videos (build/build-conference-data.py). Fields the source does not carry are absent from the payload, and therefore absent from the page.
