A reason to come here rather than to YouTube

Five drafts of the page every one of the archive’s 1,212 videos will be published on. YouTube already shows the tape. These pages show what YouTube cannot: what was said, to the second and searchable; who and where it speaks of; the series it belongs to and the week it was made in; and where else in fourteen years the same words were said.

Is a call to action appropriate here?

Only the one the video itself makes

Yes — but not a donation box, and not on every video. The weekly dialogue is a live broadcast with a question-and-answer, and its own YouTube description invites viewers in: “Send your questions, thoughts and reports to questions@schillerinstitute.org or ask them in the live stream.” Someone who has just watched 39 minutes of it is the most likely person on the whole site to take part in the next one. So the drafts turn that sentence — quoted, never rewritten — into the page’s one call to action, with the weekday the series has actually aired on (measured: a Tuesday in 42 of the 51 weeks before this episode).

Where the record carries no invitation, the next step is the next episode: a finished series such as Daily Beethoven is best watched in order, so it offers No. 32. Where there is neither, there is no call to action at all. Registration, donation and membership stay in the header, where Blocksy puts them.
