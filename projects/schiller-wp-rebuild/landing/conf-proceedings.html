The 40th-Anniversary Season

In the Spirit of Schiller and Beethoven

The season that opened the Institute’s fifth decade closed the way every conference closes — with the chorus standing. Two days of strategy, science and music, argued as one subject: that the survival of civilization is a question of beauty.

December 7–8, 2024 Online & worldwide Two days · 6 languages

Schiller and Beethoven met once, in the mind: the poet gave the composer the words that became the anthem of a united humanity. Forty years ago this Institute was founded on their shared wager — that a people schooled in great classical art cannot long be governed by fear, and that the same lawful beauty which orders a late Beethoven quartet can order an economy, a peace, a civilization. This conference is that wager, argued for two days by people who came from six continents to make it.

The founding idea · convened by the Schiller Institute, December 2024

The Opening Statement

Why we convened

Read into the record by the chair on the first morning — the standfirst is editorial; the full text is a placeholder for the conference’s opening address.

We meet at a moment when the old order can no longer promise even its own survival, and a new one has not yet been built. Between those two facts lies the whole of our work. The temptation of such a moment is to become small — to manage decline, to count only what is lost.

We refuse that temptation on principle. The human being is not a wolf, not a consumer, not a cost to be optimized: he is, as our tradition has always insisted, made in the image of the Creator, and therefore capable of discovering the ordering principles of the universe and composing new ones in art and in statecraft alike.

For two days we will put that proposition to work — on the financial architecture, on the physical economy, on the war danger, and on the classical culture without which none of the rest is possible. And we will end, as we always do, by singing.
