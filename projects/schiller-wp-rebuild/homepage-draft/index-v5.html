Provocation through rigor, not rhetoric

Do not take our word for any of it.

The Schiller Institute has been publishing dated proposals and forecasts since 1984, and dated criticism of the prevailing order for longer than that. Some of it was ignored and then happened. Some of it was ignored and did not. Both kinds are in this record, because a record that contains only the first kind is not a record.

So the pages below are built to be checked rather than believed. Every claim on this site is anchored to a dated document you can open. Where the outcome is drawn from an outside authority — the World Bank, the IEA, the World Resources Institute, a national railway operator — that authority is named and linked at the point of the claim, including where its findings cut against us. Where our own document has not yet been located in the archive, the entry says so in plain sight rather than going quiet.

There are two ways in. They meet in the same place.

The legacy archive

Four decades already online, in three different states of repair.

archive.schillerinstitute.com holds material going back to the founding. Some of it has been re-typeset into the new library; some exists only as a page scan; a good deal has not been migrated at all. Rather than quietly dropping the third category, every record in this library declares which of the three it is — so a reader who hits a scan knows it is a scan, and a reader who finds nothing knows the gap is ours and not theirs.

Classical culture · Why it is in the same building as the economics

Beauty as a theory of mind

The claim is not that art is uplifting. It is that the act by which a composer resolves a dissonance and the act by which a scientist overturns an axiom are the same act — and that a society which cannot do the first will stop being able to do the second. If that claim is wrong, the Institute's two halves have no business under one roof. It is an epistemological position, and it is arguable.

On the controversy

You have probably read something unflattering about this organisation.

Very likely you have. The Schiller Institute and the movement around it have been the subject of hostile press, litigation, and serious criticism over four decades, and some of that criticism is substantial rather than lazy. We would rather you read it here, with our answer beside it, than discover it later and conclude we hoped you would not look.

This page will list the principal criticisms with links to the strongest version of each, and answer them one at a time — conceding where a concession is owed. It is being written now and is not yet published; when it is, it will be linked from this paragraph.
