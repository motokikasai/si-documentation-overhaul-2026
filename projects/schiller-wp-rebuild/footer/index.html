A footer, six ways

Drafts of the footer that will sit under every page of the new site, all built on Jasper and all pointing at the URLs the content model actually serves. Round two is the current proposal; round one is kept for reference.

Round two · the current proposal

Fewer words, equal faces, one language control

Answering the review: the transparency links lost their paragraphs, the eight language chips became one compact switcher, every portrait is now the same size, and each draft gives its bands deliberately unequal weight so none competes. D's carousel is a plain slide — the 3D was dropped — and the line under every name is now a real job title, never a year span.

  1. DThe Rotunda

    A slow right-to-left strip of equal medallions — a plain marquee, nothing rotating or scaling. The line beneath names whoever is passing, or whoever you point at.

  2. EThe Cabinet

    The Schiller line beside a wall of framed portraits travelling past a lit centre. The middle picture comes forward, takes its colour back and is named.

  3. FThe Quiet

    No portraits, no motion, almost no words. One line with the air around it, the archive as a contents page opposite, everything else on one rule.

  4. ≡Side by side

    All six stacked at full width, scrolled to the footer, with a table of what each is best and worst at.

Round one · for reference

  1. AThe Colonnade

    A portico of twelve faces from the archive opens the footer, then four columns of real archives, then a “Check us” rail in its own band.

  2. BThe Ledger

    The archive as an audited table: every line a count, every count a link to the list it counts, with a dated source note and the Impressum in full.

  3. CThe Envoi

    A cited line of Schiller at banner size, then three doors — read it, hear it, come and argue — each with a face and its numbers.

What all six hold to

  • Always night.The footer is site chrome, not the page, so Jasper's “one dark band per page” does not bind it — and a page may still spend its own band in the body.
  • Roles, not colours.The footer re-points --si-ink, --si-card, --si-rule … onto night once. Every si- component then works inside it unchanged.
  • Brass exactly once.One mark per footer: the portico's rule, the ledger's total, the third door, the ring's eyebrow, the quote's rule.
  • Eight languages.The WPML switcher is part of the design, not an afterthought. Round two folds it into one control that works by pointer, keyboard and type-ahead, and is a plain list of links with no script.
  • No face is bigger.Every portrait in round two is the same size. Position in a carousel means where somebody is, never how much they matter.
  • A name always carries a role.The line under a face says what that person does, sourced in build/titles.json — never “In the archive 2012–2014”. Someone with no sourced title is simply not in the cast.
  • Hover changes one thing.The portrait goes from the archive's grey treatment to colour. No ring, no border, no lift.
  • Checkable.Every number is sourced and dated, the Impressum is reachable, and the primary material — recordings, PDFs, signatory lists — is one click away.
  • No invention.Details the Institute has not supplied are visibly marked as placeholders rather than filled in plausibly.
